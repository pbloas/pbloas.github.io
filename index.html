I am a doctoral researcher at the Department of Geography of the Universidad Complutense de Madrid.

My research focuses on social vulnerability to the green transition in the Madrid metropolitan region, examining how climate adaptation policies reshape spatial inequalities, employment opportunities, and social cohesion. Methodologically, I combine spatial analysis with Computational Social Science tools.

I hold a Master's degree in Computational Social Science from the Universidad Carlos III de Madrid.

Projects

  • INNOMET-MAD: Socioeconomic innovation and metropolitan governance in response to environmental change in the region of Madrid

    MICIU/AEI · Ref. PID2024-156170OB-C31 · 2025–2028 · Predoctoral researcher (FPI)

    Madrid subproject of INNORESMET (Adaptation and socioeconomic responses to environmental change in metropolitan contexts), a coordinated project with the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona and the Universitat de València (Generación de Conocimiento 2024 call). It studies the socioeconomic conditions for climate mitigation and adaptation in the Madrid metropolitan area, from green and circular-economy employment to urban climate governance, and compares them with other Spanish metropolitan areas (Barcelona, Valencia and Zaragoza) to draw lessons for collaborative metropolitan policy. Principal investigators: Simón Sánchez-Moral and Rosa de la Fuente.

  • The Politics of Clean Streets: Urban Maintenance and Voting in the City of Madrid

    Replication materials · Master's thesis, Computational Social Science (UC3M) · 2019 & 2023 municipal elections · MIT license

    Census-tract dataset for the 21 districts of Madrid that links street-cleaning intensity to turnout and party vote shares in the 2019 and 2023 municipal elections. The cleanliness index is a length-weighted average of the City Council's four street-cleaning levels (December 2021), and a crosswalk reconciles the census-tract boundaries changed between 2019 and 2023. Controls include income, population, age, education and origin (INE and Madrid City Council), plus litter-bin and streetlight density as proxies for urban activity. The pipeline outputs a GeoPackage and comes with the Quarto notebooks for data preparation and analysis, figures and tables.
